Education systems compared

How school and university stages are organised in West Bengal and India, Bangladesh, England, Scotland, Germany and the USA. This is a guide to structure, not a qualification-equivalence table: two qualifications taken at a similar age are not automatically equal, and syllabuses can differ a lot.

Who decides what counts. Only the university you apply to (and, where relevant, the national recognition body, such as UK ENIC) decides whether your qualification meets its entry requirements. Always check the entry requirements on the course page. Wales and Northern Ireland are not covered yet.

Recent changes

  • West Bengal Higher Secondary is semester-based. WBCHSE Notification L/PR/176/2024 (18 April 2024) split Class XI–XII into four semesters: Class XI from 2024–25, Class XII from 2025–26. Semesters I and III use multiple-choice questions; II and IV use short and descriptive answers. The Council conducts Semesters III and IV. [1]
  • CBSE has a new curriculum for 2026–27. Released 1–2 April 2026; it brings the NCF-2023 scheme of studies into Class IX. [2]
  • Indian bachelor's degrees can now be 3 or 4 years. Under the UGC undergraduate framework, a student can leave with a certificate after 1 year, a diploma after 2, a degree after 3, or an Honours (or Honours with Research) after 4. Each university adopts it separately: Bankura University, for example, from 2023–24. [3]
  • Indian master's: 1 or 2 years. The UGC postgraduate framework (June 2024) allows a 1-year master's after a 4-year Honours degree (160 credits) and a 2-year master's after a 3-year degree (120 credits). Universities implement it in their own regulations. [4]
  • Scotland has a new qualifications body. Qualifications Scotland replaced the SQA on 1 February 2026. National 5, Higher and Advanced Higher courses continue. [9]
