Students abroad: the numbers

How many students from India and Bangladesh study abroad, where, and in which subjects. Every figure below names what it measures, the period it covers and its original source.

Four different measures — never add them together.
Enrolments: students registered at a university at a point in time (HESA, Destatis, Open Doors).
OPT participants: in the USA, graduates on Optional Practical Training work permits. Open Doors counts them in its totals although they are no longer attending classes.
Visa grants: visas issued in a year (UK Home Office). Not the number of students in the country.
Government estimates: India's Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) compiles counts from its missions, including school pupils.

India: how many, and where

MEA, as on 1 January 2025: 18,82,318 Indian students abroad in 153 countries. This total includes 6,28,305 school pupils; the figure for universities and other tertiary institutions is 12,54,013 (12.54 lakh). The MEA table gives no notes on how missions collected the numbers [1].

Where do Indian students study, by subject?

Two different kinds of information sit side by side below, and they must not be confused:

  • What is published about Indian students. Only a few broad figures exist. DAAD India's German shares — engineering incl. biology-related fields 60%, social sciences/economics/management 21%, mathematics/natural sciences 18%, other 6% — add up to 105%, so they cannot be read as separate slices of one whole until DAAD explains the overlap [11].
  • Illustrative examples, not enrolment. Universities that QS ranks highly for the subject in 2026 [23]. This says nothing about how many Indian students attend them. Rankings favour large research universities; many excellent, more affordable options rank lower.

What data exists at university and subject level

  • UK (HESA): publishes students by university and by country of permanent address, and separately by university and subject. It does not publish the three together, so a ranking such as “universities with the most Indian chemistry students” is not available in open data; it would need a custom HESA data request [8].
  • Germany (Destatis, DAAD/DZHW): national counts of foreign students by citizenship [12] and international students by broad subject group [10]. Some universities publish their own tables of students by nationality, e.g. Saarland University [25], but there is no national university × subject × nationality table.
  • USA (Open Doors, SEVIS): totals by country of origin and by academic level [2]; fields of study and leading institutions for all international students together, not by nationality [6]. Some universities publish their own international student reports.
  • Bengali students: no country counts students by mother tongue, and India's government reports national totals only — a parliamentary question asking for state-wise figures received none [22].
